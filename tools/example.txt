== sp-solar
# Our star
The Sun is an ordinary star: a medium-sized yellow dwarf, one of perhaps 100–400 billion in the Milky Way. What makes it special is simply that it's ours. It is 1.39 million km wide, about 109 times Earth's diameter, and you could fit roughly 1.3 million Earths inside it.
In the core, the temperature is about 15 million °C and the pressure is about 250 billion times the air pressure at sea level. Under those conditions, hydrogen nuclei collide hard enough to fuse into helium. Every second the Sun turns about 600 million tonnes of hydrogen into helium, and around 4 million tonnes of mass disappear as pure energy (E = mc²).

### Why it doesn't explode or collapse
The Sun is a balancing act. Gravity squeezes inwards; the heat from fusion pushes outwards. If fusion speeds up, the core expands, cools slightly and fusion slows again. This self-regulation has kept the Sun stable for about 4.6 billion years.

### What it's made of
• About 73% hydrogen and 25% helium by mass
• The remaining 2% is "metals" to astronomers: oxygen, carbon, iron and more
• It rotates faster at its equator (about 25 days) than at its poles (about 35 days), because it isn't solid
> The light reaching you now left the Sun's surface about 8 minutes ago, but the energy behind it took tens of thousands of years to work its way out from the core.
!? The Sun loses about 4 million tonnes of mass every second, yet it's so big that over its whole life it will lose less than 0.1% of its mass this way.

# Accuracy can lie
Accuracy is the share of predictions that are correct. With imbalanced classes, it rewards ignoring the rare class. Fraud, defects, diseases and churn are all rare-event problems.
ex: 10,000 transactions, 100 fraud | Model always says "OK" | Accuracy = 9,900 ÷ 10,000 = 99%, fraud caught = 0

### Balanced accuracy
Balanced accuracy averages the recall of each class, so a model that ignores the rare class scores only 50%.
